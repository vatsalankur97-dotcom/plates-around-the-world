    Where to Eat in Lisbon

    Published 16 Oct 2026 · Updated 22 Oct 2026

    By Plates Around the World

    Custard tarts in Belém, garlic prawns at Ramiro and a market hall built for grazing: Lisbon's most-praised tables.

    Stock photo

    Researched guide: built from public ratings and press coverage, not a first-hand visit. How we research.

    Lisbon rewards an appetite: seafood, slow-cooked petiscos, and a pastry culture that's worth crossing town for.

    1. Pastéis de Belém: the original custard tart

    Baking since 1837 to a closely guarded recipe at Rua de Belém 84–92, near the Jerónimos Monastery, this bakery is the name food writers reach for when it comes to pastéis de nata. Sprinkle with cinnamon and sugar, and eat them warm.

    2. Cervejaria Ramiro: seafood feast

    A beer hall famous for garlic prawns, clams and its prego (steak sandwich) for dessert, as seen in the Lisbon episode of Anthony Bourdain's No Reservations (2012). Queues are part of the experience.

    3. Time Out Market (Mercado da Ribeira)

    Opened in 2014 inside the Mercado da Ribeira in Cais do Sodré, this food hall gathers stalls from well-known Lisbon chefs under one roof. It's a great way to sample lots in one go.

    4. Taberna da Rua das Flores

    A small Chiado tavern serving a daily-changing menu of Portuguese petiscos, at Rua das Flores 103. No reservations: a door waitlist starts once it's full (usually around 6 pm), and it's closed on Sundays. Some reviewers report it's cash only, so bring some.

    Good to know

    Lunch is often better value than dinner, and many traditional places close on Sundays.

    Sources (checked 16 Oct 2026): Pastéis de Belém official site; IMDb: No Reservations "Lisbon" (S8, 2012); Eat Like Bourdain; Wikipedia: Time Out Market Lisboa; Time Out Market Lisboa; Taberna da Rua das Flores official site. Hours change, so check before you go.
