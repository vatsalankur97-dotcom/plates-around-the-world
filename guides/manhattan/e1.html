    Episode 1 · 9 July 2025 · Tier $

    Manhattan

    One afternoon, one bus, about $79.

    We have three hours, one bus ticket and one pizza stop. This is Manhattan on a small budget, told by the clock.

    About $79 for the afternoon, per person. About $14 for three slices at Joe's Pizza, 1435 Broadway, and about $65 for a one-day hop-on hop-off ticket. About ₹6,800.

    Place · 2:20 PM

    Penn Station / W 34th St

    The first Penn Station had a main waiting room modelled on Roman baths, including the Baths of Caracalla. It was built of pink granite and torn down starting in 1963. In 1965, two years after the demolition began, the city passed its landmarks law and created the Landmarks Preservation Commission.

    Tip Moynihan Train Hall, across Eighth Ave, opened on 1 Jan 2021. It is free to walk in and much calmer.

    These are estimates, not receipts. Eater NY (25 Jun 2025) put Joe's slices between $4 and $6; the $14 figure is one cheese slice at $4 and two topping slices at $5. The $65 figure is Big Bus's Discover 24-hour ticket at full price on the site in October 2026. An archived July 2025 price was not found, and the operator on this ride is still unconfirmed. About ₹6,800 uses the RBI July 2025 monthly average of ₹86.11 per dollar (Table 204): 79 × 86.11, rounded. That July row came from a search result and was not read on the RBI page itself.

    Sources: Pennsylvania Station (1910–1963), Wikipedia · Moynihan Train Hall, Wikipedia.

    Skyscrapers lining Broadway under a blue sky with clouds

    02

    Place · 2:48 PM

    Broadway, walking north

    Broadway follows an older road, much of it the old Bloomingdale Road. The 1811 street grid was laid around it, so it cuts across at an angle. Where it crosses an avenue, it leaves odd triangles. The Flatiron and One Times Square both sit on them.

    Tip Walk this stretch. You see more at street level.

    Sources: Commissioners' Plan of 1811, Wikipedia · One Times Square, Wikipedia.

    Three pizza slices on paper plates, seen from above

    03

    Food · 3:05 PM

    Joe's Pizza, 1435 Broadway

    Joe Pozzuoli, from Naples, opened Joe's in 1975 at Bleecker and Carmine Streets in Greenwich Village. The shop is now three doors down, at 7 Carmine St. The Broadway shop, at 1435 Broadway at W 40th St, is one of the family's other locations.

    Slices ran about $4 to $6 in mid-2025. Eat at the counter and you're back out in ten minutes. Toppings other than pepperoni are unconfirmed.

    About $14 for three slices (one cheese and two topping slices). About ₹1,200. Running total about $14.

    Estimate, not a receipt. Eater NY (25 Jun 2025) put Joe's slices between $4 and $6. The $14 figure is cheese at $4 plus two topping slices at $5.

    Sources: Joe's Pizza · Joe's Pizza, Wikipedia · Eater NY, June 2025.

    Giant curved digital billboards at Times Square by day

    04

    Place · 3:22 PM

    Times Square / One Times Square

    The New York Times built its tower here, and in April 1904 the city renamed Longacre Square after the paper. The Times outgrew the tower and left its offices there in 1913. The first ball dropped from the roof on New Year's Eve 1907. The last office tenants left in 1996. Since then the tower has earned its money from the billboards on its walls. When we stood there, it was in the middle of a $500 million renovation, finished in January 2026.

    Tip Weekday mid-afternoon is a calmer daytime slot.

    Sources: One Times Square, Wikipedia.

    Stone facade of the New York Public Library with a red banner

    05

    Place · 3:33 PM

    New York Public Library & Bryant Park

    The library was dedicated on 23 May 1911, on the site of the old Croton reservoir. Its marble lions were first called Leo Astor and Leo Lenox. In the 1930s Mayor La Guardia named them Patience and Fortitude, for what New Yorkers needed to survive the Depression. Patience sits on the south side of the steps and Fortitude on the north. By the 1970s, Bryant Park behind it had been taken over by drug dealers.

    Tip Look for Patience on the left as you face the steps.

    Sources: NYPL: the library lions · Bryant Park, Wikipedia.

    Place · 3:58 PM

    Top deck of the bus, down Fifth Avenue

    Construction on the Empire State Building began on 17 March 1930. The building opened on 1 May 1931, one year and 45 days later. The frame rose about four and a half floors a week. It stayed the world's tallest building for about forty years.

    Tip Big Bus's 24-hour Downtown loop ran 9 AM to 4:30 PM, every 30 minutes, on the current timetable. Board early in the day.

    Bus, about $65. Running total about $79 (about ₹6,800).

    The Flatiron Building wrapped in scaffolding against blue sky

    07

    Place · 4:03 PM

    The Flatiron Building

    Daniel Burnham's firm designed it, and it opened on 1 Oct 1902. It has 22 storeys on a triangular block. The tip is only about 6 to 6.5 feet wide. Early on, people called it "Burnham's Folly" and bet on how far the debris would fly when the wind knocked it down. In July 2025 it was being turned into condominiums, and the scaffolding came off only in early 2026. So we saw it wrapped up.

    Tip The classic photo is from the 23rd St plaza, at the north tip.

    Sources: Flatiron Building, Wikipedia.

    Cast-iron facade with fire escapes above a busy sidewalk

    08

    Place · 4:09 PM

    Greenwich Village to SoHo

    SoHo's streets were laid out on the Bayard family's land in the 1790s. In the 1800s they filled with factories and sweatshops. In the 1960s, artists moved into the empty lofts for their high ceilings and big windows. The city protected the cast-iron district in 1973.

    Tip Greene and Mercer Streets, one block off Broadway, are quieter.

    Sources: SoHo Cast Iron Historic District, Wikipedia · Commissioners' Plan of 1811, Wikipedia.

    A tall tower with a gilded pointed roof seen through trees near City Hall Park

    09

    Place · 4:28 PM

    City Hall Park

    City Hall was built from 1803 to 1812. It is the oldest city hall in the US still doing its original job. On 9 July 1776, troops gathered on this common to hear the Declaration of Independence. That was 249 years to the day before our visit.

    Tip The park is a shady rest stop between the bus and the Financial District.

    Sources: New York City Hall, Wikipedia · City Hall Park, Wikipedia.

    Trinity Church's Gothic tower among Financial District buildings

    10

    Place · 4:34 PM

    One World Trade Center and Trinity Church

    One World Trade Center is 1,776 feet tall, a nod to 1776. The red steel sculpture in Zuccotti Park is "Joie de Vivre" by Mark di Suvero. It is about 70 feet tall and has stood here since 2006. Trinity Church dates from 1846. It was the tallest building in New York until 1890. Alexander Hamilton is buried in its churchyard.

    Tip The churchyard is a short, quiet stop right on Broadway.

    Sources: One World Trade Center, Wikipedia · Zuccotti Park, Wikipedia · Trinity Church (Manhattan), Wikipedia.

    Financial District towers near Bowling Green

    11

    Place · 4:38 PM

    Bowling Green

    Bowling Green was made a park in 1733. It is the oldest public park in the city, and its iron fence is the city's oldest fence. In 1776, after the Declaration was read, a crowd pulled down the statue of King George III, and the crowns on top of the fence posts were sawn off. The Charging Bull by Arturo Di Modica first appeared outside the Stock Exchange in December 1989. Police removed it, and it was set up at Bowling Green on 20 Dec 1989.

    Tip Go early if you want a photo with the Bull without a queue.

    Sources: Bowling Green (New York City), Wikipedia · Charging Bull, Wikipedia.

    Glass towers rising over trees in Battery Park City

    12

    Place · 4:54 PM

    Battery Park City and the Hudson

    This neighbourhood stands on land made in the river. Over 3 million cubic yards of soil and rock went in, much of it dug out to build the original World Trade Center. The glass Winter Garden at Brookfield Place was badly damaged on 9/11 and reopened on 11 Sep 2002.

    Tip The riverside path here is free and runs north along the water.

    Sources: Battery Park City, Wikipedia · Brookfield Place (New York City), Wikipedia.

    The steel arch of Pier 54 against the Hudson River sky

    13

    Place · 5:19 PM

    Pier 54 arch, West St

    That arch is all that is left of Cunard's Pier 54. In April 1912, the Carpathia brought Titanic's survivors here. In 1915, the Lusitania sailed from this pier on its final voyage. Most of the pier was demolished in 1991, and only the arch was kept. Little Island opened beside it on 21 May 2021.

    Tip Little Island is right next to the arch.

    Sources: Pier 54, Wikipedia.

    Reflective glass towers of Hudson Yards

    14

    Place · 5:24 PM

    Hudson Yards

    Hudson Yards is a 28-acre development built over the West Side Rail Yard, where trains still park underneath. The High Line ends at its north end, near 34th St.

    Tip The High Line is free. Walk it south from here.

    Afternoon total, about $79. About ₹6,800.

    Sources: Hudson Yards, Manhattan, Wikipedia · The High Line, Wikipedia.
