ARTICLE TITLE

Custard tarts in Belém, garlic prawns at Ramiro and a market hall built for grazing: Lisbon's most-praised tables.

Researched guide: built from public ratings and press coverage, not a first-hand visit. How we research.

Lisbon rewards an appetite: seafood, slow-cooked petiscos, and a pastry culture that's worth crossing town for.

1. Pastéis de Belém: the original custard tart

Baking since 1837 to a closely guarded recipe, this bakery is the name food writers reach for when it comes to pastéis de nata. Sprinkle with cinnamon and sugar, and eat them warm.

2. Cervejaria Ramiro: seafood feast

A beer hall famous for garlic prawns, clams and its prego (steak sandwich) for dessert, as made famous by Anthony Bourdain's No Reservations. Queues are part of the experience.

3. Time Out Market (Mercado da Ribeira)

A market hall in Cais do Sodré gathering stalls from well-known Lisbon chefs under one roof. It's a great way to sample lots in one go.

4. Taberna da Rua das Flores

A small Chiado tavern serving a daily-changing menu of Portuguese petiscos, frequently recommended by food media. It's no-reservations, so arrive early.

Good to know

Lunch is often better value than dinner, and many traditional places close on Sundays.

Sources: Time Out Lisbon; Anthony Bourdain, No Reservations (Travel Channel); Michelin Guide Portugal; Condé Nast Traveller; Tripadvisor and Google Maps reviews.
